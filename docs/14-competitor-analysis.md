## Defensible distinction to test

An account free, discreet appointment preparation journey that exposes the reason behind each step, separates deterministic navigation from optional AI education, and allows local user controlled sharing. Its value is in reducing uncertainty and repeated disclosure before care, not in predicting fertility better than a diagnostic service.

A consumer might choose it to prepare privately without buying a test. A clinic might use a later reviewed version to receive more structured concerns, if it actually improves preparation without inappropriate referrals or extra staff burden. Both are hypotheses. Obtain clinician feedback and a small comprehension study rather than claiming novelty.

## Weaknesses and evidence needed

The workflow is copyable, there is no exclusive dataset or clinical partner, and a carefully written leaflet may perform similarly. Clinical content permissions, local pathway maintenance and trust are operational work, not an instant moat. Evidence of improved comprehension, clinician usefulness and safe behavior would support adoption. A prospective partner pilot and ongoing governance could build defensibility; an LLM wrapper by itself will not.

Prioritize one reviewer, a tested report and honest evaluation over adding home testing, booking, chat or a multilingual claim. Later compare against actual clinic intake and navigation processes with consented observation; do not invent competitor limitations from absence on a marketing page.
