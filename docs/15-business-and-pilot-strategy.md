# Business, pilot and scale

The commercial hypothesis is clinic supported preparation and education, not consumer fertility prediction. No customer, partnership, revenue or market share is established.

| Role | Job and potential value | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| User | Understand concerns and prepare privately | Comprehension, control, less perceived disclosure effort |
| Fertility/andrology clinic or men's health provider | Receive useful history, reduce clarification burden | Clinician report usefulness, staff time and appropriateness of presentation |
| Private hospital / healthcare network | Consistent safe education before consultation | Governance, pathway integration and procurement case |
| Preventive health program | Reach people missing early information | Equitable voluntary reach and verified follow through |
| Employer wellness buyer | Could fund general education | High conflict/privacy risk: no identifiable assessment, no individual employer reporting or employment use; defer |

## Business model comparison

Clinic licensing is the recommended pilot hypothesis: a fixed fee for maintained reviewed content and a private preparation workflow. Per provider subscription or network B2B SaaS could follow if operational value is proven. White label may help distribution but multiplies review, localization and support costs. Consumer subscription has weak fit for an episodic task. Referral commissions, sponsored ranking and per test incentives risk unnecessary referrals and are excluded.

No pricing or revenue forecast is claimed. Validate willingness to pay through a small number of clinic interviews after the prototype, not a fabricated total addressable market. Keep navigation independent of payment incentives and disclose commercial relationships. A patient should not receive a specialist recommendation merely because that clinic pays.

## Proposed pilot progression

| Stage | Scope and exit evidence | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Hackathon | Fictional demo, engineering tests, formative comprehension, clinician review if available | No patient deployment claim |
| Partner discovery | 3 to 5 clinician/operations interviews, actual workflow and buyer problem | Explicit authorization before outreach; none sent |
| Protocol design | One clinic, defined adult audience, local pathways, intended use and safety lead | Clinical, legal/regulatory, ethics/research determination, contracts and rights review |
| Supervised feasibility | Approximately 20 to 30 consenting adults, sample size hypothesis not powered clinical trial; clinician reviews navigation | Approved protocol, incident process, data processing agreements, secure operations |
| Adoption decision | Report usefulness, comprehension, safety events, burden, equitable reach and follow through feasibility | Do not claim fertility improvement without suitable prospective study |

Pilot duration could be 6 to 8 weeks after approvals, an assumption rather than a committed schedule. Real approval/procurement may take months. No deployment date should precede those gates.

## Operating cost scenarios

| Cost driver | Prototype planning assumption | Pilot consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting/domain/metadata limiter | Allow $0 to $50/month combined, verify Vercel/Upstash plans; no purchase authorized | Region, access, logs, support and reliability can increase costs |
| AI | Formula in document 10; $20 to $50 experiment allowance | Actual tokens, retries and vendor terms matter more than demo estimates |
| Clinical review | 4 to 8 reviewer hours assumed, availability and rate unknown | Recurring review, incident oversight and local pathway upkeep |
| Translation | Excluded before deadline | Qualified medical translation plus consumer/RTL checks |
| Security/legal | Team design work only in prototype budget | External assessment, processing contracts, insurance and incident response not priced |
| Support/maintenance | One builder, prior 25h/week assumption; Jeremiah contribution unassigned | Named operator and maintenance budget required, not a free extension of hackathon work |

For 10,000 short explanation calls the simple token scenario is only a few dollars, but that is not total cost of care delivery. Staff review, governance, contracts, hosting and support can dominate. Do not extrapolate gross margin or annual revenue from token cost.

## Scale and adoption risks

Future clinic records require an approved region and transfer map, tenant isolation, managed authentication, durable consent/sharing, report versioning and auditable human review. Add PostgreSQL when persistence solves a real need; do not put clinic records into the hackathon architecture by stealth. FHIR integration requires actual partner profiles, codes, conformance tests and provenance.

Success metrics: correct understanding of limits/next step, useful preparation report, inappropriate referral burden, adverse events, staff clarification effort, consent withdrawal success, equitable completion and opt in follow through. Referral count alone is not success. Commercial uncertainty includes episodic demand, competitive clinic forms, procurement time, liability, IP terms and guideline reuse restrictions.
